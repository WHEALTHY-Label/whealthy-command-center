Ignacio "Iiggie" José Zambrano, II

Founder & Steward

Can I be honest with y'all?

I'm from Mississippi. I'm not a doctor. I'm not an influencer. I'm not trying to be famous. I'm somebody who was crashing at 2 PM every single day, giving my family the worst version of myself, and finally decided to do something about it.

Here's the moment it broke me open. My daughter looked at me one night and said, "Daddy, you're more fun in the morning."

She was right. By 6 PM I was done. Not physically. I could still move. But mentally? Emotionally? I was the ghost at my own dinner table. Scrolling my phone while my family ate. Short with my wife. Present in the room and absent everywhere it counted.

And the crazy part is, I thought I was doing everything right. I was working hard. Providing. Showing up to church on Sunday. But my mornings belonged to gas station coffee, my afternoons belonged to the fog, and my evenings belonged to whatever was left, which was nothing.

So I tried to fix it the way everybody tries to fix it.

I cut coffee cold turkey. The headaches had me back on it in three days, worse than before. I bought the expensive focus pills. Felt nothing but lighter in the wallet. I even built one of those 45-minute morning routines. Meditation, journaling, cold shower, the whole thing. Lasted 11 days. Then life happened, and it never happened again.

Every fix failed for the same reason. I was treating a pattern problem like an energy problem. I kept trying to add fuel to a system that had no governor on it.

That's the shift. The day I stopped asking "how do I get more energy" and started asking "who's actually governing the energy I already have," everything changed. My faith had been telling me this the whole time. Don't be conformed to the pattern the world hands you. Be transformed by renewing the way you operate. I just had never applied it to my mornings.

So I built a rhythm instead of a ritual. Five minutes, not forty-five. Morning governed. Midday held steady. Afternoon reset without the third cup or the gas station run. It didn't compete with my life. It lived inside my life, in the moments I was already drinking something, already eating, already reaching for something at 2 PM.

The routine didn't give me more energy. It gave me back my evening. It gave my daughter the 6 PM version of me that used to only exist at 6 AM.

I refined it, I shared it, and the first 50 people who ran it with me weren't influencers. They were pastors, coaches, nurses, truck drivers, parents. People carrying real weight across faith, family, and work. Not one of them joined because of marketing. They joined because they watched me do it and said, "I want that."

That's the whole story. No lab coat. No six-pack. Just a steward who found something that worked and can't not tell people about it.
